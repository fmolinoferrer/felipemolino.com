Governing an AI Workforce

Decision rights, evidence and earned autonomy: how the Control Tower Method grows from a way to plan and measure AI-built software into one governed operating loop that runs the product and the growth of a company, and starts itself every night.

Felipe MolinoFounder, CorkAIFollows Part I: The Control Tower Method

Executive summary

  • Part I set the method: split a product into control areas, score each from L0 (not done) to L5 (autonomous), and give agents authority only where the evidence supports it. Part II is about running a company on it: who decides what, what evidence each decision needs, and what happens when the loop starts itself.
  • One loop, two lanes. The same operating system runs the product lane (build, test, release) and the growth lane (plan, produce, approve, publish, measure). Both share one control tower, one evidence trail and one owner.
  • The work starts without me. Each night agents read production signals, failing tests and campaign results, and propose fully scoped work. Agents build, test, document and produce.
  • I keep a handful of decisions: what gets built, what gets merged, what reaches customers, and what gets promoted with money. Each arrives with the evidence I need to decide in minutes.
  • The first full engineering cycle moved 19 changes into production code in one day, with zero failing tests reaching me. A rehearsal that combined changes before merging caught five conflicts the per-change checks had missed.
  • Autonomy is earned, never assumed. L5 within 6–9 months comes from track record and pre-approved classes of change, not from removing the gates.

A team that only acts when someone remembers to ask is a tool. A team that senses, proposes, builds and reports on its own, under rules it can't break, is a workforce.

03One loop, two lanes

A control area can sit in the product (billing, sign-in), in the engineering system (testing, releases) or in growth (content, paid acquisition). The ladder doesn't care which. So the same loop that builds the product also grows it: the same sensing, the same packets, the same gates, the same evidence trail.

Product lane

Build, verify, release

  1. Signals become scoped work items overnight
  2. Agents build and test; CI gates every change
  3. Batches are rehearsed before they merge
  4. The owner releases with a packet; a live check follows

Growth lane

Plan, produce, measure

  1. A rolling 6–8 week content plan, re-planned every month
  2. Agents produce each week's posts from the owner's own material
  3. The owner approves one week at a time; agents schedule only what's approved
  4. Every post is measured at 24 hours, 48 hours and 7 days, and traced to sign-ups

Outcomes, not activity

An audit of past campaigns showed they had been optimized for clicks, not sign-ups. The fix was the same as in engineering: measure the outcome, and gate decisions on it. Every post carries a tracked link, every sign-up records where it came from, and the weekly score is accounts created and taste quizzes completed, not likes. Rules decided in advance (boost only what already outperforms, stop any ad after two weeks, never let one ad take most of a month's budget) turn judgment into gates, exactly as CI does for code.

04The controls that make it trustworthy

Part I introduced review packets and decision records. Part II adds the controls a business needs before it lets a loop run overnight.

06Results from the first cycle

  • A roadmap that behaves like an operating system: 12 themes, about 100 work items in one format, and a 10-week plan sized to real capacity, all read by the control tower from GitHub.
  • 19 changes integrated in a day. Rehearsed together, merged in dependency order, ending with a green main branch: about 2,500 tests and the browser journeys passing.
  • The checks paid for themselves at once. New suites found authorization and data-integrity defects, which were fixed before release, including a pagination edge case that could hide a customer's items.
  • Mobile layout is now a gate. Recording the product for the growth lane surfaced layout problems on narrow phones; each became a fix with a browser test at three phone widths that blocks any regression.
  • Owner decisions are recorded once and reused everywhere. A day of product decisions became a dated log the agents now plan from, and the growth lane runs from the same kind of log.

08The next horizon

The loop is built to scale beyond one owner. These are the next controls on the roadmap, each designed to widen what agents may do on their own while keeping the evidence trail intact.

  1. A separate agent identity with platform-enforced approvals. A dedicated identity makes "only the owner approves" a rule of the platform itself.
  2. Staging and automatic rollback. A copy of production to release into first, and an automatic roll back when the live check fails.
  3. AI quality gates. Golden datasets scoring every model and prompt change on accuracy, cost and p95/p99 latency before it ships.
  4. Service-level objectives. Explicit targets for the journeys that matter (scan, chat, checkout), with error budgets that decide when feature work slows down.
  5. Install attribution on every platform. Crediting app installs on iPhone and Android to the post or ad that drove them, so growth decisions use the full funnel.
  6. Predictive planning. Once enough posts are logged, a model predicts each planned post's reach and conversion, the weekly plan is ranked on it, and its accuracy is tracked every week.
  7. Pre-approved classes of change. A written policy for which changes may merge on green after a probation period. This is the real path to L5.
  8. One audit trail of approvals. Who approved what, when, and on what evidence, across code, releases, content and spend, ready for investors, partners and compliance.
  9. Control drills. Rehearsed rollbacks and kill-switch tests each quarter, so the controls are known to work.

09What this means for any leader adopting AI agents

Know which decisions are yoursAnd what evidence you need to make each one in minutes. "I review everything" wastes the agents' speed; "nothing" leaves the risk unmanaged.
Let the work start itselfAgents that sense and propose on their own turn a tool into a workforce.
Earn autonomy one class of change at a timeOn the strength of a measured track record, exactly as with a new hire.
Run one loop across the companyGrowth, support and operations can run on the same gates and evidence as engineering, with autonomy earned per area.

Part I argued for building the factory first. Part II is what it looks like to run a company on it: a product that improves every night, a growth engine that learns every week, and one accountable owner.
