About

Twelve years of turning ambiguous mandates into measurable outcomes.

I'm an AI, product, and engineering executive with 12+ years in logistics and supply chain technology. I manage an eight-figure vendor and partner portfolio alongside NFI's President of Transportation Management, VP of Innovation, and VP of Procurement, and I cut RFP and continuous-improvement turnaround from six weeks to one.

On the engineering side, I built an internal platform and infrastructure team from 0 to 20+ across six countries, and was part of the leadership team for a 50+ engineer offshore staff-augmentation engagement (about $1M a year), where many of the engineers reported to me directly.

The work I'm proudest of is the unglamorous kind. I led a six-month phased production migration off an expiring platform license — running live and validation workloads in parallel, then cutting over account by account across ten enterprise accounts with no service interruption. I also lead an agent-authored development program producing 80% of the team's pull requests at 90%+ adoption, backed by hundreds of golden datasets and regression tests as the safety net.

Outside of NFI I build my own AI products end to end. The Control Tower Method

Roadmaps, gates and maturity: how I move a product's control areas from manual to autonomous, with multiple AI agents working under controls.

Felipe MolinoFounder, CorkAI · Senior Director of Engineering
Multiple agentsClaude Code agents (Opus 5.5, medium effort), each on bounded, controlled work
27control areas mapped on NFI's digital-twin platform
L0 → L5an evidence-based ladder from not done to autonomous
~1,150automated tests on every change at CorkAI
100%of CorkAI's API routes covered by contract tests
~370story points on a 10-week plan
6–9 motarget to reach L5 where it pays

01At a glance

Every product runs on controls: the checks, reviews and decisions that keep it working. Most are manual. AI agents can do that work, but an agent without controls is just a faster way to break things.

The framework

Split the product into control areas and score each from L0 (not done) to L5 (autonomous). An agent gets more authority in an area only when the evidence supports it.

What I built

At NFI: 27 control areas on an enterprise platform. At CorkAI: the whole system, from roadmap to tests, with multiple Claude Code agents doing the building.

Results so far

The gates are enforced and the tests already caught real defects before release. Most of the foundation is in review, not yet released.

The end game

Where failure is expensive, agents detect, fix and document known problems inside limits I approved in advance. People handle strategy and exceptions.

The model writes the code. The operating model decides whether the code is any good, and how much an agent is trusted to do on its own.

02The framework

A control area is anything the product depends on that can fail without anyone noticing: a feature like billing or sign-in, or part of the engineering system like testing or releases. Each area gets a current level, a target and the evidence behind both. Not every area needs L5.

03What I built

How decisions get remembered

Agents start every session with no memory, and people move on. So every decision that's expensive to reverse gets an architecture decision record (ADR), at NFI and at CorkAI. Agents read the relevant ADRs before they work, so settled decisions aren't reopened, and whoever comes next inherits the why, not just the code.

04Results so far

The CorkAI foundation was built in one intensive working session, and most of it is in review, not yet released. What's already true:

✓The gates are real. Every pull request carries a review packet, and CI rejects any that don't.
✓The tests paid off on day one. They found authorization and data-integrity defects, fixed before release.
✓Every API route has a contract test, so an agent can't silently break the mobile apps.
✓The plan can't drift from the code. Roadmap, board and metrics read from one system of record.
✓Key decisions are written down, drafted by agents and approved by me.

05The end game

Not everything at L5: every area at the level its risk justifies, with evidence to prove it. For CorkAI that's L5 within six to nine months where a silent failure is expensive, and L3–L4 elsewhere.

What L5 looks like

That's the end state for CorkAI, and it's the same direction for NFI's 27 control areas: controlled agents run the controls, and people own strategy, approvals and the exceptions.

06What this means for a product org

A maturity matrix per areaFixed L0–L5 definitions, a target per area, evidence that moves with the work.
Review packets for every changeWhy, what, effect, tests and rollback, whether a person or an agent made it.
ADRs as shared memoryAgents draft them, people approve them, and everyone reads them before working.
Autonomy earned per areaAgents get authority the way a new hire does: with a track record.

Build the factory first. Then let the agents build the product, and let the evidence decide what ships.
